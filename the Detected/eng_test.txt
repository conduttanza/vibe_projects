Pip Fitz-Amobi, a gifted high school student from the town of Fairview, selects a murder case that happened in the local community for her senior project. Andie Bell, a popular teenager, was murdered five years ago, and her boyfriend Sal Singh was suspected of murder; he subsequently committed suicide. 
The town's general assumption is that Sal was guilty of the crime. Pip does not believe this version and conducts an autonomous investigation to get to the bottom of things.
She reads through archival reports, peruses evidence available, and interviews individuals connected to Andie and Sal. 
Her results indicate inconsistencies in the original investigation that were not followed up on, including motives not investigated, relationship connections not made, and social relationships that conditioned how people perceived things in the community. Pip gets warned and discouraged in following her investigation but keeps gathering and contrasting data.
Through careful research, she determines that Sal was not the killer and discovers the actual culprit who had deliberately concealed their footsteps. Pip's findings are contrary to the town's suspicions and culminate in the real murderer being revealed, hence justice for Andie Bell.
Old Major, an educated and respected boar at Manor Farm, calls the animals to a meeting, denouncing humans as selfish exploiters and inspiring them with a vision of utopian change. He teaches them the revolutionary song Beasts of England, which is a metaphor for hope and solidarity. Two young pigs, Napoleon and Snowball, take over leadership after his death and begin to organize the farm for rebellion. Later, the animals turn on the farmer Mr. Jones and take over the farm, renaming it Animal Farm and following the Seven Commandments of Animalism, the most basic one being: "All animals are equal."
Initially, life on the farm is improved: work is allocated, and the animals enjoy freedom. Snowball teaches reading, whereas Napoleon secretly trains some puppies to be his enforcers. 
The pigs gain privileges for themselves, citing them as necessary for their health. Friction between Napoleon and Snowball heightens, particularly over Snowball's suggestion to build a windmill to improve the farm’s life conditions. Napoleon becomes dominant, driving Snowball off with his dogs and abolishing democratic sessions. Squealer, a proficiently speaking pig, convinces the animals that Napoleon discovered the windmill.
When the pigs become powerful, they engage in activities that are human, like re-writing history. Napoleon exercises propaganda, purges, and scapegoating to govern the animals, blaming all failures on Snowball. The new windmill is destroyed by one of the neighbouring farmers, Mr. Frederick, but Napoleon and the pigs utilize the animals' will in keeping them working. 
The pigs increasingly become indistinguishable from humans, walking upright, wearing clothes, and exploiting the other creatures. The Seven Commandments are boiled down to one cynical statement: "All animals are equal, but some animals are more equal than others." Eventually, as Napoleon dines with human farmers and reclaiming the name Manor Farm, the animals realize that he became what they swore to kill.
The Time Machine was a fictional piece of machinery thought to be a joke amongst the guests at a dinner table who are shocked to find that the time traveller, a scientist who claims to be in possession of one, proved its existence with a smaller machine that went into the future.
To further prove his extraordinary machine worked, the time traveller embarked on an enormous journey, he started by going into the future at a increasingly rapid rate as days flew by like blinking lights; he stopped at a distant year into the future, the year 802,701 AD; he saw beautiful gardens and nature and discovered a evolved human species, the Eloi.
He saves an Eloi woman, Weena, from drowning; she will follow him as a companion. It is then revealed that another species of humans’ lives in this era, the Morlocks, they stole the time machine, and they live underground, they are the people who farm the Eloi, in their surface based beautiful gardens.
The story develops around the time traveller going in a statue-like monument where his machine was brought to and in the process, he loses Weena, who until then showed him Eloi customs and traditions.
Once he gains access to his machine he travels to the furthest future Earth can offer, its final moments of life, there he encounters the most simplistic of life forms and shows an alien wasteland.
It is only then that he decides to go back to his dinner guests from the 1890s and after he tells them what he saw, they are skeptical, but he will return to the future a few days later leaving only his tale behind.
